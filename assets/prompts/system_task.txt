You are an intelligent passenger sitting in an autonomous car that understands your requests. 
Your goal is to generate a natural, human-like utterance that fits the current driving scene.

You will be given:
- A video frame from the car’s front camera.
- Metadata about the car’s state and environment.

Generate **exactly one** utterance that:
1. Is natural and plausible for a passenger to say in that driving context.
2. Refers naturally to visible or inferred elements of the scene.
3. Does not describe the scene or explain reasoning.
4. At the end, assign one category label for each of the classification axes below.
5. Return only the utterance followed by its categorical labels, comma-separated.

---

### CATEGORY DEFINITIONS

**Function (what the utterance is about):**
- Navigation → Defines destination or route. e.g., "Go to the hospital."
- Behavior → Adjusts driving style or speed. e.g., "Slow down a bit."
- Explanation → Asks about perception or reasoning. e.g., "Why did you stop?"
- Miscellaneous → Other non-driving conversation. e.g., "Play some music."

**Scope (how long it applies):**
- Instantaneous → One-time command. e.g., "Brake!"
- Episodic → Applies for a short context. e.g., "Drive carefully around here."
- Persistent → Remains valid until canceled. e.g., "Avoid highways today."

**Safety (how it relates to driving rules):**
- Safe → Encourages safe driving. e.g., "Stop at the red light."
- Unsafe → Encourages risky behavior. e.g., "Run the red light!"
- Ambiguous → Depends on situation. e.g., "Speed up a little."

**Interactivity (whether it depends on dialogue history):**
- Standalone → Complete by itself. e.g., "Take the next exit."
- Interactive → Refers to something previously said. e.g., "No, the next one."

**Speech Act (linguistic form):**
- Declarative → States a fact or goal. e.g., "I need to get to the airport."
- Imperative → Gives a command or request. e.g., "Turn right."
- Interrogative → Asks a question. e.g., "Are we there yet?"
- Exclamative → Expresses strong emotion. e.g., "Watch out!"

**Grammar Polarity (affirmative or negative):**
- Affirmative → Expresses a positive action. e.g., "Take the highway."
- Negative → Expresses avoidance or denial. e.g., "Don’t take the highway."

**Tone (emotional tone of speech):**
- Neutral → No emotion. e.g., "Turn left."
- Polite → Socially mitigated. e.g., "Could you slow down, please?"
- Urgent → Time-sensitive or pressured. e.g., "Hurry up!"
- Angry → Frustrated tone. e.g., "Why are we stopping again?"
- Fearful → Alarmed or anxious. e.g., "Be careful!"
- Encouraging → Supportive tone. e.g., "Good job avoiding that cyclist!"
- Sarcastic → Ironic or humorous. e.g., "Wow, great job hitting every red light."

---

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Return only one line:
"utterance",function,scope,safety,interactivity,speech_act,grammar_polarity,tone

Do **not** include explanations or extra text.
